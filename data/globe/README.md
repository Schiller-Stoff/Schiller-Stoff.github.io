# DERLA globe data

`derla-globe.js` is one self-contained data file for a DERLA globe page. It
assigns `window.DERLA_GLOBE` and contains no `fetch`, no image and no external
reference, so it works from `file://` with a single script tag:

```html
<script src="data/globe/derla-globe.js" defer></script>
```

The same run also writes three static artefacts beside it: the 720 × 380
fallback map, the 96 × 96 locator globe and the nine state-table rows. All
four files are generated by `tools/globe/build_globe.py`; never edit them by
hand. The German titles and addresses are DERLA's, verbatim, and are not
translated.

## Structure

```
window.DERLA_GLOBE = {
  meta: {...},
  world:  { names, codes, polys },
  states: [ {id, name, nameEn, count, terrorCount, share, centroid, polys} ],
  points: { lon, lat, title, address, pidSuffix, layer, type, date, public, state,
            layers, types, dates, publics }
}
```

### Geometry convention

A `polys` value is an array of polygons; each polygon is an array of rings; each
ring is a flat array `[lon, lat, lon, lat, ...]`.

- The first ring of a polygon is the outer ring; any following rings are holes.
- Outer rings are counter-clockwise and holes clockwise (positive/negative
  signed area), so a renderer can fill with the nonzero rule and stroke the same
  rings.
- Rings do not repeat their first vertex. The renderer closes them.
- Coordinates are WGS84 longitude/latitude, unprojected. The renderer projects
  the sphere; orthographic is the intended globe.
- Quantization: points 4 decimals (≈11 m), states 3 (≈100 m), world normally 2 (≈1.1 km); a sub-pixel microstate may keep a 3-decimal emergency ring.

### `meta`

`meta` carries the source and licence, the two dates, `counts`, the projection
and assignment notes, the simplification notes, the detail template and the
disclaimer. `meta.detail` documents the pid packing:

- `points.pidSuffix` is the DERLA pid without the common `o:derla.` prefix.
- Full pid = `meta.detail.pidPrefix + points.pidSuffix`.
- Detail URL = `meta.detail.url` with `{pid}` replaced by the full pid.

`meta.yearRule` states the date rule: `points.date` indexes `points.dates`; a
year is the date string when it matches `^(18|19|20)\d\d$`, otherwise null.
This avoids a redundant 4,393-value year array; the raw date strings, including
`-`, `5/46` and `klar`, are preserved in `dates`.

### `world`

`names`, `codes` and `polys` are parallel arrays of 242 countries, sorted by
name. `names` are Natural Earth's English `ADMIN` names; `codes` are ISO A3
with `ADM0_A3` as the fallback. Shapes are simplified at 1.3° worldwide, 0.35°
inside the box 5–25 E / 42–55 N (so Germany, Czechia, Slovakia, Hungary,
Slovenia, Italy, Switzerland and Liechtenstein stay recognizable), and 2° for
Antarctica. Outer rings below 0.03 deg² are omitted outside the Europe box, but
every country keeps at least one ring; sub-pixel states are a three-point
triangle rather than an empty polygon. The only surviving hole at this scale is
Lesotho inside South Africa. Antarctica keeps its outline; its ring includes the
-90° edge, where all longitudes meet, so a projection must not draw that as a
straight screen line across the globe.

The world is a decorative background, not a political map. It does not
adjudicate disputed boundaries beyond Natural Earth.

### `states`

Nine states, sorted by ISO 3166-2. `name` is German, `nameEn` English.
`count` and `terrorCount` are record counts; `share` is `count / 4393` (all
records, not only assigned ones). `centroid` is `[lon, lat]`, area-weighted and
checked to fall inside the state; it is not projected.

Lower Austria carries Vienna as a hole (two rings); Tyrol has two polygons
(North and East Tyrol). The outlines are simplified at 0.006° (~0.7 km) and
quantized to 3 decimals. State borders are simplified independently, so shared
borders can differ by up to about 0.7 km (well under a pixel at 600 px); this
is not a topological dataset.

### `points`

One entry per DERLA record, in DERLA's map order, 4,393 entries. The record id
is the 0-based array index; it is stable for this extract. Where present,
`pidSuffix` is the stable external id.

- `lon`, `lat`: numbers, 4 decimals.
- `title`, `address`: strings; `address` is null for six records.
- `pidSuffix`: string or null (377 nulls).
- `layer`, `type`, `date`, `public`: indices into `layers`, `types`, `dates`,
  `publics`.
- `state`: index into `states`, or null for the four records outside Austria.
- `layers` (30), `types` (35), `dates` (93), `publics` (3) are the string
  tables. `dates` contains null, `-`, `5/46`, `klar` and the year strings.

`publicAvailable` has three values: 4,203 `Öffentlich zugänglich`, 174
`Nicht öffentlich zugänglich`, 16 null; the table is the authoritative list.

Coincident records are kept one per array entry; no record is merged. At
4-decimal precision 4,210 distinct positions remain, so a renderer can group
visually, but the record count stays 4,393.

## Point-in-state assignment

1. Point-in-polygon (ray casting) against the **full-resolution** Natural Earth
   1:10m admin-1 polygons, subtracting holes. 4,376 records fall inside exactly
   one state.
2. Records outside all nine polygons are assigned to the nearest state only if
   the distance is ≤ 5 km **and** the address names no other country. 13 records
   qualify (the farthest is 2.24 km; most are under 1.2 km): 7 in Styria
   (Klöch, Bad Radkersburg, Leutschach, St. Anna am Aigen), 2 in Salzburg
   (Krimmler Tauern), 1 in Tyrol (Erl) and 3 in Vorarlberg (Thal bei Sulzberg,
   Lustenau ×2).
3. Four records are genuinely abroad and keep `state: null`:
   - `9430 St. Margrethen, Schweiz`
   - `9434 Au, Schweiz`
   - `9470 Buchs, Schweiz`
   - `88131 Lindau, Deutschland`

The 13 border assignments are a nearest-geometry fallback for records that the
generalised NE 1:10m outline misses; their addresses place them in the named
state. Nothing is clipped or dropped.

## Static artefacts

Three static files sit beside the data file. They are generated by the same
run, from the same extract, and `--check` compares all four byte for byte.

| file | raw / gzip bytes | what it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `globe-fallback.svg` | 32,456 / 10,455 | 720 × 380 inline fallback map at the Austria pose |
| `globe-locator.svg` | 5,467 / 2,480 | 96 × 96 inline locator globe |
| `state-rows.html` | 1,080 | nine `<tr>` rows for the no-JS state table |

All three are UTF-8 with a final newline; gzip is zlib level 9 with `mtime=0`.
None of them loads a script, a font, an image or an external reference.

### `globe-fallback.svg`

One inline-ready SVG, `viewBox="0 0 720 380"`,
`preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"`, `role="img"` and a `<title>`/`<desc>`
pair. The projection is orthographic, centred on 47.65 N / 14.15 E, north-up.
The national outline is fitted to the viewBox with 8% padding on the limiting
axis: the screen box is 57.60,35.17–662.40,344.83, so the horizontal margins
are exactly 8% and the vertical margins are larger because the country is wider
than the 720:380 box.

Paths, in paint order:

| class | content |
| --- | --- |
| `globe-fallback__state` | nine paths, `data-state="AT-1"`…`AT-9`; 11 subpaths in total |
| `globe-fallback__outline` | one national border, 531 points |
| `globe-fallback__dots` | 1,379 deduplicated memorial positions |
| `globe-fallback__terror` | 87 deduplicated sites of terror |

State subpaths: AT-1 1, AT-2 1, AT-3 2 (Lower Austria plus the Vienna hole),
AT-4 1, AT-5 1, AT-6 1, AT-7 2 (North and East Tyrol), AT-8 1, AT-9 1. The
build prints each state's subpath, point and byte counts.

The dots and squares are zero-length subpaths (`M x,y h0`), rounded to whole
viewBox units and deduplicated per `(x, y, class)`. 4,393 records collapse to
1,379 dot marks and 87 square marks at this scale; the prose keeps the record
count and the 4,210 4-decimal positions. The host stylesheet sets `fill: none`,
a stroke and `stroke-linecap: round` for dots and `square` for terror, so print
and forced colours can re-ink them. There are no inline fills, strokes or styles, no
`<style>`, no script and no id beyond `globe-fallback-title` and
`globe-fallback-desc`.

The national outline is traced from the Natural Earth 1:10m state rings:
shared borders are stored as the same vertices, so quantizing to 1e-6 and
dropping every edge seen twice leaves one national ring. It is generated from
the state data, not a second hand-maintained outline. The outline is a
stroke-only path; the host sets `fill: none` and the border stroke, then fills
the state paths beneath it.

### `globe-locator.svg`

A 96 × 96 orthographic locator on the same pose and projection: a 46-unit limb
circle, the visible world land (outer rings only, clipped to the hemisphere and
simplified to 1 px; 152 segments, 795 points) and Austria as
`globe-locator__austria` (7 points). The classes are
`globe-locator__limb`, `globe-locator__land` and `globe-locator__austria`;
there is no fill or stroke in the file. The land path is intended to be
stroked, not filled, because clipped rings end on the limb. The host sets
`aria-hidden="true"`: this is a quiet atlas inset, not an interactive control.
The file carries a `<title>`/`<desc>` and no id beyond those two.

Budget: 5,467 bytes raw / 2,480 gzip, below the 6 KB target. No graticule is
drawn; the limb, the land and the Austria highlight are the whole inset.

### `state-rows.html`

Nine rows, in AT-1…AT-9 order, with the English `nameEn`, the record count, the
share of all 4,393 to one decimal and the sites-of-terror count:

```html
<tr class="globe-states__row" data-state="AT-1"><th scope="row">Burgenland</th><td>230</td><td>5.2%</td><td>1</td></tr>
```

The nine shares are 5.2, 4.3, 22.2, 2.6, 12.5, 8.6, 5.3, 4.6 and 34.6 %; they
sum to 99.9 % because four records are outside Austria and keep `state: null`.
There is no button, no note row and no wrapper: the host inserts the fragment
into its own `<tbody>` and enhances it if it wants to.

## Regeneration

From the repository root, with the saved inputs:

```bash
python3 tools/globe/build_globe.py \
  --derla /tmp/opencode/derla-clean.geojson \
  --natural-earth /tmp/opencode/ne0.geojson \
  --admin1 /tmp/opencode/ne10-admin1.geojson
```

The defaults already point at those three cache paths, so
`python3 tools/globe/build_globe.py` is equivalent. One run writes all four
files — `derla-globe.js`, `globe-fallback.svg`, `globe-locator.svg` and
`state-rows.html` — into `data/globe/`. Use `--out-dir` for a scratch build and
`--check` to rebuild in memory and compare every committed file:

```bash
python3 tools/globe/build_globe.py --check
```

`--check` prints `OK`, `STALE` or `MISSING` for each of the four files, reports
sizes and exits non-zero if any differs. It writes nothing. The budgets are
600,000 raw / 160,000 gzip / 40,000 world / 15,000 states / 55,000 fallback /
6,144 locator bytes.

To put one artefact somewhere other than `<out-dir>`, use its own flag; the
defaults are `<out-dir>/globe-fallback.svg`, `<out-dir>/globe-locator.svg` and
`<out-dir>/state-rows.html`:

```bash
python3 tools/globe/build_globe.py \
  --fallback /tmp/opencode/globe-fallback.svg \
  --locator /tmp/opencode/globe-locator.svg \
  --state-rows /tmp/opencode/state-rows.html
```

Other options:

- `--fallback PATH`, `--locator PATH`, `--state-rows PATH` override the three
  artefact paths. The SVGs and rows are still regenerated on every build.
- `--map-html PATH` extracts from a saved copy of DERLA's map page (4,399
  features, six malformed coordinate pairs reported and dropped) instead of
  the cleaned GeoJSON.
- `--fetch` downloads the official sources when an input path is missing. It is
  an explicit opt-in, never a default, so a committed build is reproducible
  from saved files.
- `--build-date YYYY-MM-DD` changes `meta.built`; the default is fixed at
  2026-11-16 so two runs are byte-identical without reading the clock.

The live map page was checked on 2026-11-16: 4,399 features, the same six
malformed pairs, and the resulting file was byte-identical to the committed one.

## Determinism

- Country and state order, string tables and all keys are sorted or fixed.
- The build date is a constant unless explicitly overridden.
- Gzip size is measured with `mtime=0`; the committed raw file is compared
  byte for byte.
- Two independent runs produced the same four files:
  - `derla-globe.js` `f6c20a52636675d64ec53418579d2763b6fd9e9794753c4c6238192dc9e51c8d`

## Limitations and anomalies

- Positions are approximate geocodes of documented places, not survey data.
  DERLA is the authoritative record; where this file and DERLA differ, DERLA is
  right.
- The extract is a snapshot, not a complete inventory of National Socialist
  persecution in Austria. The 4,393 records are the ones on DERLA's public map
  on 2026-11-13.
- Six features on the live map page have malformed coordinates and cannot be
  placed: one `[48.149028, 48.149028]` (Simmeringer Hauptstraße 244, 1110 Wien)
  and five Salzburg records with latitudes such as `47805794.0` or longitudes
  such as `13016757.0`. Five look mechanically repairable by a decimal shift;
  the sixth does not. They are excluded from the cleaned input, so the file
  stays at 4,393 and matches the published storymap. `--map-html` lists them
  on every extraction rather than dropping them silently.
- 377 records are DERLA's "Überlappende Zeichen" overlap placeholders. They
  have no pid of their own (the source's `places` array lists the underlying
  records), so `pidSuffix` and the detail link are null. They are kept as
  records, one per position, because DERLA's map counts them.
- Six records have no address (five at the Krimmler Tauern, one Vienna plaque).
  They are kept with `address: null`.
- 516 records have no year: 234 null, 279 `-`, 2 `5/46`, 1 `klar`. The raw
  date strings are preserved in `dates`.
- Four records are outside Austria (three Switzerland, one Germany) and keep
  `state: null`; they are not clipped.
- World shapes are a globe-scale simplification, not a survey boundary. Small
  islands below 0.03 deg² outside the Europe box are omitted; every country
  keeps at least one ring. Sub-pixel countries may be a triangle. Only
  Lesotho's hole survives at 2-decimal precision; Vatican and San Marino holes
  collapse at this scale.
- State outlines are simplified independently, so shared borders are not
  topologically snapped.
- No images or image URLs are included. Detail links go to DERLA.

## Why the data is packed this way

The titles and addresses alone are about 341,000 characters, and a verbose
object per record would exceed the raw budget. The file therefore uses parallel
arrays plus string tables for the repeated layer, type, date and access values,
and stores the pid without its common `o:derla.` prefix. The year is derived
from the date table rather than duplicated. This keeps the whole page
functional with **one** script tag and no second request; a base/details split
was unnecessary once the pid prefix and the redundant year array were removed.

If you change the input or the geometry constants, rerun the generator and
`--check`; do not patch `derla-globe.js` by hand.
